### The corrected reading, rule by rule (reading 3)

This is the one rule list. Slice 1's throwaway program implements it independently; this slice builds it into the product.

- **R1. Frontmatter.** Unchanged: the existing walk of consecutive leading `---` blocks (lines 434–457).
- **R2. Code fences.** Every body line after the frontmatter is read for fences, inside a left-out section or not. Let t be the line with surrounding whitespace removed (the existing `trim()`).
  - Opening, outside a block: t begins with a run of three or more backticks, or three or more tildes. For a backtick run, no backtick may appear anywhere in t after the run (the CommonMark rule for an info string). The block's character is that character, its length the run's length, its line the line's 1-based number in the file.
  - Closing, inside a block: t consists only of the block's character, at least the block's length of it. Indentation and trailing whitespace do not matter (the trim removes them).
  - Inside a block, every other line is content, including a run of the other character, a shorter run, and a run followed by an info string such as `js`.
  - The opening and closing lines are content too: kept or left out like any other line.
  - From the opening line through the closing line, no line is a heading: it neither starts nor ends a left-out section.
- **R3. An unclosed block.** When a block is still open after the last line, the reading has no result: `{ hash: null, ok: false, reason: 'unclosed-code-fence', line: <the opening line>, frontmatter: null }`.
- **R4. Ending a left-out section.** Outside a block, a heading-shaped line (the existing `headingLevel(t) > 0`, line 354) whose level is at most the open section's level ends it: at any indentation and at any level, level 1 included (the owner's answer to "Does a level-1 line end a left-out section": it does, as today).
- **R5. Starting a left-out section.** Outside a block, a heading-shaped line starts a left-out section only when all three hold:
  - (a) its level is 2 or deeper;
  - (b) the characters before its first `#` are spaces and tabs only, measuring fewer than four columns, where a space adds one column and a tab moves to the next multiple of four (the CommonMark tab stop); a line led by any other character that `trim()` removes, such as a no-break space, cannot start a section;
  - (c) its normalised title (the text after the `#` run, trimmed and lowercased, as line 470 does) equals one of the seven names of `EXECUTION_SECTIONS`, or begins with one of them immediately followed by a space (U+0020) or an opening parenthesis.
  - The mismatch explanation's extra titles (exact match, as line 471 does) also start a section only under (a) and (b).
- **R6. Checkbox lines.** Dropped wherever they stand, inside a block or not, exactly as today (`isCheckboxLine`, lines 377–385, applied at line 478).
- **R7. The digest.** Unchanged: the length-prefixed, separator-delimited sha256 of lines 498–503.

**Reading 2** is today's walk exactly: no blocks, any heading-shaped line at any level and indentation starts a section when its title begins with one of the seven names (`isExecutionHeading`, lines 338–343).
